## 1. Why the project existed in 2020

James started hoping to write this game in 2020 after playing Alessandro Roberti's *A Legionary's Life*.

The 2020 plan was to imitate ALL's format:

- a fixed sequence of events
- abstract turn-based combat

That was a constraint plan, not a taste plan. James was going to do the coding by hand and did not think he had the ability to go further. Under those constraints the surface would have been text, still images, and very basic animation.

The 2020 README sentence still on `master` is:

> RPG game set in Iron Age Spain, where the player starts as a Celtiberian tribesman who must side with the Romans, Carthaginians, or Iberians

That sentence is the stub. It is not cancelled. James's comments today sit on top of it.

## 2. What changed

James now has agentic coding systems. He does not have to stay inside the 2020 box of text, stills, and very basic animation. He wants the project to be more ambitious than a straight ALL clone when the tools can carry that.

He did not name an engine, a GUI toolkit, or a map technology in this session.

## 3. What does not change

The game stays in the perspective of an individual Iberian soldier. That is the through-line from ALL. Hispania is still a personal war-RPG, not a grand-strategy player-as-state.

## 4. Who the soldier is

James wants to prioritize the **non-Celtic Iberians**. He called them more distinctive. If the focus were Celtic peoples as such, that might be a separate project.

**Celtiberians can eventually be in the scope of Hispania.** They are not banned. They are not the first people to design around.

This revises the *priority* of the 2020 README's "Celtiberian tribesman" start. It does not write a new starting *oppidum*, year, or people-name. Those are still open.

Restatement, not extra invention: "Iberians" here means the non-Indo-European communities of the east and south (the README's third option), as opposed to the Celtiberian cluster on the Meseta and as opposed to a generic Celtic roster. James did not name Contestani, Edetani, Bastetani, Ilergetes, or anyone else today.

## 5. Shape of the world

Long term, James would like Iron Age Iberia treated more like an **open-world environment** than like ALL's railroad of parts.

Short term, he said a **fixed sequence of events might be easier to design and build**.

So the build order he actually spoke is:

1. Keep the individual-soldier view.
2. Accept a fixed sequence as the nearer thing to design and ship first.
3. Point the project at an open-world Iberia once that is possible.
4. Do not stay stuck on text-and-stills because 2020 forced that.

He did not define what "open world" means in systems language (hex map, free travel, living *oppida*, simulated seasons, etc.). Do not fill that in from Teutoburg or Suvorov on the strength of this file.

## 6. Relationship to *A Legionary's Life*

ALL remains the inspiration for the *perspective* (one soldier, a war that will replace you) and for the 2020 *format* (fixed sequence, abstract turn-based combat).

ALL is not a ceiling on presentation anymore.

ALL's Roman career ladder is still the wrong clone target. James is not asking to play a legionary with an Iberian sprite. He is asking to play an Iberian soldier in Iberia.

## 7. What this file does not decide

- Engine, language-beyond-what-already-exists-on-the-branch, or GUI.
- Combat resolution (ALL stance-and-menus vs something larger).
- Permadeath and next-man inheritance.
- The three-way allegiance as three campaigns vs one map with a fork.
- Starting year, home settlement, or which Iberian people is first.
- When the open-world pass starts.
- Whether the existing `src/hispania/` slice on this branch stays, changes, or waits. James said documentation this session.
